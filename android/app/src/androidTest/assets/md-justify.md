# A Deliberately Long Markdown Chapter Heading Written So That It Must Wrap Onto Several Rendered Lines

Justification is a typographic operation that distributes the slack of a line into the spaces between its words, so that the right edge of a paragraph arrives flush against the measure instead of trailing off in a ragged fringe. It is an old convention and a quiet one: nobody notices a justified column until they see an unjustified one beside it, at which point the ragged edge looks less like a choice and more like an oversight.

A heading is not body prose. A heading that happens to be long enough to wrap onto a second and a third line must not be stretched flush on both edges, because a stretched heading reads as a paragraph rather than as a title, and the widened word gaps look like a rendering defect rather than a typographic decision.

## A Second Long Markdown Heading, Also Written To Be Long Enough That It Wraps Across More Than One Rendered Line

The lamplighter walked the length of the harbour road every evening, and every evening the same gulls wheeled above the same grey water, complaining about the same indifferent weather. He had counted the lamps once, when he was young enough to think counting them mattered, and had arrived at a number he no longer trusted.

What interested him was not the lamps but the intervals between them, the stretches of unlit pavement where the light from one post had given up and the light from the next had not yet taken hold. In those intervals the harbour looked older than it was, and the boats at their moorings became shapes rather than boats.

### A Third Long Markdown Heading Kept Long Enough To Wrap So The Exclusion Is Observable At Every Level

By the time he reached the end of the road the lamps behind him had settled into their steady yellow and the ones ahead were still deciding. He turned around, as he always did, and walked back through the intervals in the opposite order, which made them different intervals entirely, and arrived home with the distinct impression of having taken two separate walks.

The gulls, who had been there for the whole of it, offered no opinion on the matter and went on complaining about the weather until it was fully dark, at which point they stopped complaining about the weather and started complaining about the dark instead.
